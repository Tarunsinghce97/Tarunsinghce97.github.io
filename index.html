Research

My doctoral work asks how to formulate viscoelastic constitutive models that remain valid at large strain and respect thermodynamics. It moves from the structure of rheological networks, to specific models, to equations that can be used to characterize real materials.

Published
IJES 2025

Rheological connections at finite deformation

I extended series and parallel rheological connections to finite deformation using multiple natural configurations. The connections are characterized through the distribution of stress power, which gives a route to building rheological networks beyond the small-strain setting.

Published
IJSS 2026

Thermodynamically consistent finite viscoelasticity

Finite-deformation viscoelastic models within the theory of evolving natural configurations, covering Maxwell, Zener, Poynting–Thomson and Kelvin–Voigt type behaviour. Particular attention goes to the driving (configurational) force associated with parallel rheological structures. The same formulation gives constitutive equations for anisotropic response.

Published
JMPS 2026

Scalar conjugate pairs and material characterization

A deformation mode-specific framework built on scalar, conjugate stress–strain pairs. A QR-based decomposition of the kinematics yields scalar governing equations for selected loading modes. These support analytical solutions and physically meaningful parameter identification for soft materials.
