Research

My doctoral work asks how to formulate viscoelastic constitutive models that remain valid at large strain and respect thermodynamics. It moves from the structure of rheological networks, to specific models, to equations that can be used to characterize real materials.

Published
IJES 2025

Rheological connections at finite deformation

I extended series and parallel rheological connections to finite deformation using multiple natural configurations. The connections are characterized through the distribution of stress power, which gives a route to building rheological networks beyond the small-strain setting.

Published
IJSS 2026

Thermodynamically consistent finite viscoelasticity

Finite-deformation Kelvin–Voigt type models within the theory of evolving natural configurations, in a stress-space formulation where the rate of dissipation is a convex function of the configurational (driving) forces. Maxwell and Kelvin–Voigt behaviour appear as limits of the Poynting–Thomson and Zener models. The Poynting–Thomson model reproduces polymer data up to a stretch of 9 over stretch rates of 0.2 to 400 s−1.

Published
JMPS 2026

Scalar conjugate pairs and material characterization

A deformation mode-specific framework built on scalar, conjugate stress–strain pairs. A QR-based decomposition of the kinematics yields scalar governing equations for selected loading modes. These support analytical solutions and physically meaningful parameter identification, validated on VHB 4910 elastomer and porcine brain tissue.

A deformation mode-specific constitutive framework for finite viscoelasticity based on scalar, conjugate stress/strain pairs: Applications to characterization of soft materials

T. Singh and S. Paul

Journal of the Mechanics and Physics of Solids, 219 (2027), 106895. Online 31 October 2026 Published

Key points

A viscoelastic framework for soft polymers and biological tissues built on scalar, conjugate stress/strain pairs from a QR (upper-triangular) decomposition of the deformation gradient. Each pair corresponds to one deformation mode (dilatation, squeeze or shear). Finite Maxwell, Poynting–Thomson and two Zener models are derived, with analytical solutions for homogeneous problems. A semi-analytical identification procedure gives parameters with direct physical meaning, and the model is validated against VHB 4910 elastomer and porcine brain tissue data. A noise study shows the procedure is stable up to about 5% noise. Keywords: viscoelasticity, finite strain, elastomers, biological tissues, QR kinematics.

Constitutive modeling of Kelvin–Voigt type viscoelastic solids undergoing finite deformations using the theory of evolving natural configurations

T. Singh and S. Paul

International Journal of Solids and Structures, 341 (2026), 114336 Published

Key points

Kelvin–Voigt type viscoelastic solids within a Lagrangian framework, using a stress-space formulation in which the rate of dissipation is a convex function of the configurational forces. The Maxwell and Kelvin–Voigt models are recovered as limiting cases of the derived Poynting–Thomson and Zener models. Integration algorithms are given, and the Poynting–Thomson model matches polymer data in uniaxial tension up to a stretch of 9 and over stretch rates from 0.2 to 400 s−1. Relaxation behaviour, rate effects and an extension to other material symmetry groups are discussed.

On the extension of the concept of rheological connections to a finite deformation framework using multiple natural configurations

T. Singh and S. Paul

International Journal of Engineering Science, 212 (2025), 104267 Published

Key points

Series and parallel connections of rheological elements are modelled within the multiple natural configurations framework, without introducing new configurations. The two connections differ only in how stress power is split: by the ratio of rate-of-deformation norms for a series connection, and by the ratio of driving-force norms for a parallel connection. The paper shows why earlier finite-deformation treatments of parallel connections are problematic, and illustrates the theory on a standard linear solid, an elastic–perfectly plastic solid and an elastoplastic solid with hardening.
